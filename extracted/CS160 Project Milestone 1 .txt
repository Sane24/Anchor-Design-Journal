Project Milestone 1: Needfinding
1. Experience You Are Planning to Redesign:
We are redesigning the experience of starting and completing work for people with mental health
challenges such as ADHD, anxiety, or depression. Our focus is on how people regulate focus,
motivation, and emotions through virtual systems, physical spaces, and personal routines, and
how these supports can be improved to reduce overwhelm and make progress feel more
achievable.
2. Needfinding Overview
We recruited non-CS 160 students and recent graduates with ADHD, anxiety, depression, or
similar mental health challenges. These participants were chosen because they regularly navigate
demanding work and school responsibilities while managing their mental health, which makes
them representative of the users we hope to support. Our redesign aims to provide them with
positive motivation, task guidance, and stress management.
During each session, we observed how the participants began working on a real work or study
task in their natural environment. Some participants allowed us to take notes on their home work
space. We then conducted semi-structured interviews around their productivity habits, challenges
when starting and completing tasks, sources of stress, and strategies and tools they use to stay
organized.

Reed Yalouh Session 1: Jacob
Description:
A recent UC Berkeley electrical engineering graduate transitioning from externally structured
student life into a more self-directed period. He is currently looking for a job and is highly
motivated by meaningful technical projects, but struggles to begin tasks when he does not see a
compelling purpose or reason to do them.
Observations:
● His mornings currently have little external structure. After waking up, he often scrolls
through social media and takes roughly 30 minutes to get out of bed.
● Without a meaningful task or clear next step, he is more likely to turn to immediately
rewarding activities such as YouTube, social media, talking with friends, or online chess.
● Tension: External obligations made him feel less free as a student, but the absence of
external structure after graduation leaves him with less direction.
● He was significantly more productive when working on his invention because he cared
deeply about the project and naturally used task lists to maintain momentum.
● Breakdown: Organization alone does not create motivation. He can know what needs to
be done, record it clearly, and still avoid beginning it.
● Workaround: He uses the desktop Notes app as a lightweight project-management
system to identify dependencies and prioritize tasks by importance.
● His workspace itself is relatively simple and functional. The larger barrier appears before
work begins: without purpose, urgency, or a clear next action, immediate gratification
becomes more appealing.
Workspace Observation:
He took us through his room, his primary workspace, and showed us how he uses the desktop
Notes app to organize tasks, dependencies, and priorities. The contrast between his current
unstructured job-search period and his highly motivated work on his invention suggested that
purpose and a clear next action matter more to him than elaborate productivity systems.

4. HMW Questions
1. HMW help students reduce external distractions, such as from social media, when they
are trying to focus on urgent work or academic tasks?
2. HMW help students break large, ambiguous projects into clear, manageable steps?
3. HMW help students reduce cognitive overload that disrupts their focus before they begin
procrastinating?
4. HMW help students overcome the mental barrier of starting overwhelming tasks?
5. HMW lower the barrier of starting unscheduled and open-ended tasks that have no clear
start or end?
6. HMW help students create meaningful progress tracking systems that reflect genuine
effort and achievement?
7. HMW build social accountability to help someone follow through on tasks they would
otherwise avoid?
8. HMW help people manufacture real urgency when a task has no immediate deadline?
9. HMW help people find their way back to a task after getting pulled into a distraction?
10. HMW help someone design around a moment right before avoidance wins, instead of
relying on willpower once they are already in it?
11. HMW help students separate academic focus from external financial or personal anxieties
so they can engage more clearly with their work?
12. HMW turn a student’s social circle into an active motivational engine rather than a
passive comfort zone to get the task done faster or started earlier?

5. Solution ideas
1. Turn distracting apps to gray-scale, and keep the essential apps in color. (HMW 1, HMW
10)
2. Buddy system with photo or screenshot progress sharing (matching anonymous or with
friends). Replaces self checked boxes with something a friend can actually see. (HMW 7,
HMW 6)
3. Growth garden where verified sessions grow faster than self reported ones. Keeps the
reward system but doesn’t let it be gamed or fakeable the way Habitica or Finch do, and
adds an accountability aspect. (HMW 6, HMW 12, HMW 8)
4. 3 main tasks to focus on a day (HMW 5, HMW 4, HMW 3, HMW 2)
5. Weekly and morning briefings pulled from gmail, slack, calendar. Turns scattered
“something is due” dread into one bounded plan. (HMW 5)
6. If-then rule builder tied to a “danger window” where users name their own triggers and
actions they can take to overcome it. If I start doomscrolling, I will set a 5 minute timer to
reset. (HMW 10, HMW 9, HMW 1)
7. Tiny first step generator on every task. Break a task down to one opener to start with.
(HMW 5, HMW 3, HMW 4)
8. Physical sticky note prompts. App suggests writing just the first step on paper and
placing it somewhere visible, externalizing the task instead of relying on memory and
adding cognitive load. (HMW 5, HMW 3, HMW 11)
9. 60-second text-prompt where the user writes out all of their current academic and/or
non-academic worries (like rent or financial aid) to keep them in a hidden tab for the
session and prevent distractions (HMW 11, HMW 3).
10. Browser extension that connects to a music streaming platform and only plays when the
user is active on their assigned task page. If they navigate away to a distracting site, the
music immediately stops playing, warns them to go back to work, and plays a distinct
timeout alert sound. (HMW 9, HMW 10)
